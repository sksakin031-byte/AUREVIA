01 · The concept

A space shaped
by its brief.

A small, light-framed retreat that treats the existing trees as the architecture's first organizing element.

A garden pavilion concept that brings its mature orchard into the plan. The purpose of this study is to show how a design idea can respond to the needs of a fictional place without losing its atmosphere.

02 · Challenge & response

The constraint
became the cue.

A clear spatial question creates a more useful starting point than a decorative solution.

The challenge

A narrow site and mature trees left little room for a conventional footprint.

The response

A slim timber frame bends around the existing oaks, with glass and slate making the threshold between shelter and garden feel continuous.

03 · Design objectives

A few clear
priorities.

These fictional objectives guide the concept from the first plan to the finishing palette.

  1. —Respect the existing trees
  2. —Keep the new footprint visually light
  3. —Make indoor and outdoor use feel connected

04 · Space planning

Let the plan
do the quiet work.

A slender linear plan bends around the existing tree positions and keeps the route from sheltered interior to garden readable.

05 · Materials & finishes

Tactile, calm
and connected.

The palette is presented as a concept direction, not a specification for construction.

  • Charred timber
  • Clear glass
  • Slate
  • Natural stone
